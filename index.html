Highlights

Four faces of one unforgettable city

Shanghai refuses to be just one thing. Give it a few days and it will show you all of its sides.

Iconic

The Bund & Lujiazui

Stand on the historic Bund promenade at dusk and watch the colonial-era facades glow gold while Pudong’s towers light up across the river. Then cross over for the observation decks of Shanghai Tower and the Oriental Pearl Tower — the postcard view, in real life.

Heritage

Yu Garden & Old Town

A 400-year-old Ming-dynasty garden of zigzag bridges, koi ponds and carved pavilions, tucked inside the buzzing Old Town bazaar. Come early, then graze your way through the snack streets outside.

Local life

The French Concession

Plane-tree avenues, art-deco villas, hidden cafes and boutiques. This is where Shanghainese go to slow down — rent a bike, follow Wukang Road, and stop wherever the coffee smells good.

Food

A city you can taste

Crispy spring rolls, xiao long bao soup dumplings, scallion oil noodles, crab-season everything. Shanghai’s food scene runs from century-old breakfast stalls to Michelin-starred dining — bring an appetite.

Itineraries

How many days do you have?

Two ready-made routes. Steal them, remix them — there is no wrong way to do Shanghai.

Day 1

Icons & river lights

  • Morning: Yu Garden & Old Town bazaar
  • Afternoon: Nanjing Road walk to the Bund
  • Evening: Huangpu River night cruise
Day 2

Old lanes, new art

  • Morning: French Concession by bike (Wukang Road, Tianzifang)
  • Afternoon: Shanghai Museum on People’s Square
  • Evening: Dinner in Xintiandi’s shikumen lanes
Day 3

Future Shanghai

  • Morning: Shanghai Tower observation deck, Lujiazui
  • Afternoon: M50 art district or a tea house break
  • Evening: Maglev to Pudong Airport at 431 km/h
Day 1–2

The classic, unhurried

  • Follow the 3-day route above at half speed
  • Add a morning food tour: dumplings, jianbing, soy milk
  • Sunset drinks with a Bund view
Day 3

Water town escape

  • Day trip to Zhujiajiao: canals, stone bridges, tea houses
  • 40 minutes by metro — a thousand years back in time
  • Back for a late dinner in the French Concession
Day 4–5

Go deeper

  • Suzhou day trip: classical gardens & silk (25 min by bullet train)
  • Propaganda Poster Art Centre & local markets
  • Your pick: cooking class, acrobatics show, or one more skyline night
Good to know

Before you book the flight

The practical stuff, honestly told. Visiting China is easier than you think — with a little prep.

🛂

Visa, simplified

54 countries (US, UK, Canada, Australia, most of Europe) get 240-hour visa-free transit via Shanghai when flying on to a third country — that’s 10 days, no consulate visit. Otherwise apply for a tourist (L) visa a few weeks ahead.

🌤️

When to go

April–May and September–November are the sweet spots: mild, clear, photogenic. Summer is hot and humid; winter is damp and chilly but crowd-free.

🚇

Getting around

The metro has English signage everywhere and goes nearly everywhere. The Maglev hits the airport in 8 minutes. DiDi (China’s Uber) works with foreign cards.

💳

Paying for things

Alipay and WeChat Pay now link foreign bank cards — set them up before you fly. Carry some cash as backup; cards work in hotels and malls.

📱

Staying connected

Google, WhatsApp and Instagram are blocked. Download offline maps, a translation app and anything you need before arrival, and consider an eSIM or roaming plan.

🗣️

Language & manners

English is limited outside hotels — a translation app goes a long way. Learn nǐ hǎo (hello) and xièxie (thank you); smiles are universal currency.

Travel rules change. Visa policy, entry requirements and app availability were accurate at the time of writing — always double-check with your airline and local Chinese consulate before you travel.
